Visual Cipher Bench: Benchmarking LLM Agents on Long-Horizon Visual Reasoning

Deyao Hong, Jiayi Jiang, Yuchun Chen, Yixuan Shi, Wenyi Li, Zheng Jiang, Chengxing Xie, Mingyu Su, Qingle Liu, Mingju Gao, Yizhe Chi, Youjie Zheng, Calvin Xiao, Kaisen Yang, Huan-ang Gao · 2611.16861 · 2026-11-12 · General
Planning / reasoning

While language-model agents excel at long-horizon coding and terminal tasks, this success relies on two conditions: explicit text specifications and immediate execution feedback. However, neither condition holds in real-world problems where critical information exists only in images, leaving it unclear whether long-horizon capability transfers from text to visual reasoning. In such visual settings, because rules and clues resist text transcription and lack test-case feedback, agents must repeatedly inspect images, simulate spatial transformations, and autonomously cross-check clues. Despite this challenge, existing evaluations fail to capture such capability: visual reasoning benchmarks largely reduce tasks to single-turn, short-chain question answering, whereas agent benchmarks focus on text-based environments or interactive states that permit trial-and-error. To bridge this gap, we introduce Visual Cipher Bench, a collection of 40 original visual puzzles across four categories: spatial manipulation and simulation, logical constraints and planning, rule induction, and knowledge-based decoding. Each puzzle provides only one to three black-and-white line drawings and a minimal submission instruction, requiring agents to discover the underlying solving mechanism entirely from the images. Across 29 reasoning-effort configurations of eight frontier models, even the strongest model, GPT-6 Astra (max), achieves only 26.7% accuracy, while five of the eight models score below 1%. Yet on a ten-task subset, providing a single non-spoiler mechanism hint boosts accuracy from 7.8% to 55.6%. This sharp contrast shows that the primary bottleneck of current agents lies not in executing multi-step plans, but in autonomously discovering rules from visual evidence. Visual Cipher Bench thus provides a rigorous benchmark for measuring long-horizon visual reasoning in agents.
