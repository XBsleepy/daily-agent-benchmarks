sudo L7: Can Coding Agents Act Like Staff Engineers?

Nick Heiner, Ian Niebres, Suhaas Garre, Sushant Mehta, Edwin Chen · 2611.30517 · 2026-11-23 · Coding / SWE
Coding agents

Coding agents can now turn a well-specified ticket into a patch that passes its tests. Experienced engineers also notice risks the request never mentioned, question flawed premises, reuse what already exists and say plainly what they did and did not check. We present sudo L7, a benchmark of 60 software-engineering tasks written by staff-level engineers, mostly in private production codebases and each built around a place where a frontier model failed. A task pairs a realistic, underspecified request with a hidden, expert-authored rubric of atomic criteria (1,287 in all), tagged with eight behavioral dimensions from functional correctness to truthful reporting. An agentic grader reads the agent's transcript, inspects and runs its code, and treats test output as evidence. An attempt passes only if it meets every criterion of the two highest severities, and more than half of these gating criteria test something other than functional correctness. On the public leaderboard as of November 2026 (19 models, each in its developer's harness or in OpenCode, three attempts per task), Claude Fable 5.2 passes 52.2% of attempts, Claude Sonnet 5.5 50.6% and Claude Opus 5.5 46.1%. GPT-6.1 Sol, Claude Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra pass 31-34%, and the other 13 models at most 18.9%. In a human-label study, three candidate model graders each agreed with a software engineer on 85-86% of criterion verdicts, about as often as two engineers agreed with each other. Cases from the benchmark show how one unsupported claim can fail an otherwise strong attempt and illustrate two sides of engineering judgment: flagging hidden hazards and avoiding needless work.
